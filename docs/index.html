2026
Thesis

Portfolio Optimization under Dynamic Preferences

Iván García Mestiza

Abstract / details

The classical stochastic control problems related to investment portfolio optimization under utility functions—as introduced by Merton—have been extensively studied across various scenarios. However, their primary limitation lies in the assumption that the utility function remains constant over time, which fails to model realistic situations. This thesis addresses the study of stochastic processes capable of capturing the evolution of investor preferences over time. Specifically, we focus on “forward-performance processes”, analyzing their key characteristics and exploring their connection to partial differential equations. Using utility functions that incorporate stochastic components, we derive explicit optimal investment strategies linked to nonnegative solutions to the heat equation. Furthermore, we examine probabilistic representations of these strategies—modeled as Brownian motions conditioned to follow an asymptotic distribution—and investigate their relationships with mixtures of points on the Martin boundary and random changes of drift. These results offer new financial interpretations of the portfolio optimization problem under dynamic preferences, while establishing a link between partial differential equations, portfolio optimization theory, and conditioned stochastic processes.

Preprint

Dynamic preferences with long-term conditioning noise

Iván García Mestiza, Daniel Hernández Hernández

Abstract / details

Preprint submitted to SIAM Journal on Financial Mathematics (November 2026).

This paper aims to analyze the role that certain future beliefs in the noise process play in the evolution of investor preferences and the forward performance process. The key characteristics of these processes are analyzed, and their connection to the Martin boundary of the heat operator. For utility functions incorporating stochastic components, explicit optimal investment strategies linked to solutions to the heat equation are derived, motivated by the fundamental work by Musiela and Zariphopoulou. Probabilistic representations of these strategies are analyzed—viewing them as Brownian motions conditioned to follow an asymptotic distribution—and the relationships involving mixtures of points on the Martin boundary and random shifts in drift are examined. These results offer a financial interpretation of the portfolio optimization problem with dynamic preferences and establish an interesting link between portfolio optimization theory, conditioned stochastic processes and PDEs, extending beyond the results already established in this field.

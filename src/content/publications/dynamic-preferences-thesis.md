The classical stochastic control problems related to investment portfolio optimization under utility functions—as introduced by Merton—have been extensively studied across various scenarios. However, their primary limitation lies in the assumption that the utility function remains constant over time, which fails to model realistic situations.
This thesis addresses the study of stochastic processes capable of capturing the evolution of investor preferences over time. Specifically, we focus on "forward-performance processes", analyzing their key characteristics and exploring their connection to partial differential equations.
Using utility functions that incorporate stochastic components, we derive explicit optimal investment strategies linked to nonnegative solutions to the heat equation. Furthermore, we examine probabilistic representations of these strategies—modeled as Brownian motions conditioned to follow an asymptotic distribution—and investigate their relationships with mixtures of points on the Martin boundary and random changes of drift.
These results offer new financial interpretations of the portfolio optimization problem under dynamic preferences, while establishing a link between partial differential equations, portfolio optimization theory, and conditioned stochastic processes.

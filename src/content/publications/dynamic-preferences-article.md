Preprint submitted to SIAM Journal on Financial Mathematics (November 2026).

This paper aims to analyze the role that certain future beliefs  in the noise process play in the evolution of investor preferences and the forward performance process.
The key characteristics of these processes are analyzed, and their connection to the Martin boundary of the heat operator. For utility functions incorporating stochastic components, explicit optimal investment strategies linked to solutions to the heat equation are derived, motivated by the fundamental work by Musiela and Zariphopoulou.
Probabilistic representations of these strategies are analyzed—viewing them as Brownian motions conditioned to follow an asymptotic distribution—and the relationships involving mixtures of points on the Martin boundary and random shifts in drift are examined. These results offer a financial interpretation of the portfolio optimization problem with dynamic preferences and establish an interesting link between portfolio optimization theory, conditioned stochastic processes and PDEs,  extending beyond the results already established in this field.
